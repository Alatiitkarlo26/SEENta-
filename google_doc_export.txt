Project SEENta: Lost and Found Management System 


Project Rationale & Background
In any academic university, the daily loss of personal belongings—such as student identification cards, wallets, mobile devices, and academic materials—is a persistent challenge. Currently, many campuses rely on legacy methods to manage recovered items, such as physical paper logbooks or scattered posts in social media group chats. These fragmented approaches introduce severe operational inefficiencies:
* Information Fragmentation and Loss: Paper logs are prone to damage, misplacement, and incomplete data entry, while social media threads quickly become cluttered, unorganized, and easily buried under unrelated conversations.
* No Central Place to Look: Found items are scattered across logbooks, offices, and group chats, so owners and finders have no single place to find each other.
* Wasted Academic Time: Students and faculty are forced to make repeated, unproductive physical visits to the security office just to check if their items have been recovered, disrupting their academic schedules.
* Verification and Accountability Risks: Relying on informal chats creates vulnerability to false claims, where individuals can fraudulently acquire items that do not belong to them. Because no record is kept, it is impossible to trace who took an item once it has been handed over.








The Proposed Solution
To eliminate these inefficiencies, Project SEENta: Lost and Found Management System is proposed as a secure, centralized web application tailored specifically for the Polytechnic University of the Philippines: Biñan Campus.
Instead of relying on a staff office, SEENta lets students and faculty help one another directly. Finders post the items they found, owners claim them, and both meet in person on campus to complete the return. Project SEENta delivers critical advantages to the university community:
1. Institutional Authenticity: The platform enforces strict registration through official university email domains (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph for students and @pup.edu.ph for faculty), ensuring that only members of the campus community can access the system.

2. Automated Efficiency: Instead of manual cross-checking, the system features automated matching that instantly cross-references newly posted found items against active lost item reports based on category and location.

3. Proof of Ownership: To reduce false claims, a claimant must fill out a Proof of Ownership Claim Form with their own information and details about the item. The finder reviews the form before approving the claim.

4. Transparency and Accountability: Every returned item is recorded in the Transaction History of both the finder and the claimant, showing who handed over and who received the item. This record discourages false claims.

5. Recovery From False Claims: If an item is claimed by the wrong person, the original owner can use the Report Falsely Claimed Item feature. The item is marked as disputed and the owner can see who claimed it, so they can still get their item back.

6. Convenience Coupled with Security: Users receive notifications regarding claim updates and potential matches, minimizing unnecessary physical trips while still requiring an in-person handover on campus.




Direct Benefits to the User 
Reclaiming time and protecting personal property within the university community. By shifting away from chaotic social media group chats and error-prone paper logbooks, the system directly addresses the daily frustrations experienced by both owners and finders.
   * Eliminating Wasted Time and Effort: Instead of making repeated, unproductive physical trips to the security office just to ask if a lost ID, wallet, or phone has been turned in, users can check listings remotely from anywhere with an internet connection.

   * Real-Time Transparency and Instant Alerts: Through automated matching and notifications, users receive immediate updates when a posted item matches their lost report, drastically cutting down recovery time.

   * Trust and Safety: Because the platform requires official institutional email registration (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph for students and @pup.edu.ph for faculty), users interact within a verified community of peers. Each user's transaction history makes their activity visible, which helps reduce the risk of malicious actors and external spam.

   * A Second Chance to Recover Lost Items: If an item is claimed by someone else, the original owner can report it and trace who received it, instead of losing the item for good.

Introduction


The Lost and Found Management System is a centralized digital web application designed to help students and faculty of the Polytechnic University of the Philippines: Biñan Campus recover lost and misplaced items directly from one another.
Campus lost-and-found management typically relies on fragmented, inefficient methods such as physical paper logbooks and unorganized social media group chat posts. Project SEENta: Lost and Found Management System 




      1. Description


Project SEENta is a centralized, peer-to-peer (P2P) digital web application designed to connect students, faculty, and campus staff directly to recover lost and misplaced belongings at the Polytechnic University of the Philippines: Biñan Campus. 


Rather than relying on administrative gatekeepers or physical office bottlenecks, SEENta operates on a community-driven, peer-to-peer model: 


      * A student or faculty member who finds a misplaced item posts it directly to the platform with details and photos. 
      * A user who has lost an item browses listings or submits a lost item report. 
      * A claimant submits a Proof of Ownership Claim Form directly to the finder detailing proof of ownership and verified identity. 
      * Both users coordinate an in-person campus handover, and upon completion, the exchange is permanently recorded into each user's public Transaction History. 
      * If an item is mistakenly or dishonestly handed over to the wrong person, the true original owner can trigger the Falsely Claimed Item Dispute Report, allowing them to trace who claimed the item and 




      2. Statement of the Problem 


Campus lost-and-found management typically relies on fragmented, inefficient methods such as physical paper logbooks at guard desks or unorganized social media group chat posts. These methods present severe flaws: 


      1. Inefficiencies & Bottlenecks: Students must repeatedly visit campus security offices between classes just to check if an item has been surrendered.
      2. False Claims & Identity Ambiguity: Social media group chats allow unidentified users to claim items without accountability or records. Once an item is handed over to the wrong person in informal settings, it is impossible to trace who took it.
      3. Lack of Transparency & Transaction Records: There is no record of who found what, who claimed what, or when the handover happened, leading to mistrust and disputes.


The Solution: SEENta establishes a verified, peer-to-peer campus platform where every user is tied to their institutional university ID, every exchange is preserved in an immutable Transaction History, and true owners are empowered to contest fraudulent claims through direct Dispute & Recovery
Reporting.




Potential Clients
      * Student Users: Registered students of PUP Biñan Campus holding verified (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph) email addresses. 
      * Faculty & Academic Staff: University professors, instructors, and personnel holding verified (@pup.edu.ph) email addresses. 




      3. Benefits


      1. Direct Peer-to-Peer Resolution: Eliminates administrative delays by allowing students and faculty to coordinate returns directly. 
      2. Standardized Proof of Ownership: Replaces guesswork with a formal claim form where claimants must supply item-specific evidence, serial numbers, descriptions, or purchase/photo proof. 
      3. Transparent User Transaction History: Both finders and claimants maintain a verifiable activity log of all items posted, claimed, and handed over, creating community accountability. 
      4. Falsely Claimed Item Recovery: True owners can dispute false claims, immediately revealing the verified institutional identity of the person who claimed the item to facilitate recovery. 
      5. Campus Safe-Exchange Zones: Encourages students to complete handovers at designated, hightraffic, CCTV-monitored campus locations. 




      4. Scope and Delimitation


In-Scope Features 
      1. Institutional Domain Authentication: Restricts registration strictly to official PUP email addresses bound to student/employee IDs. 
      2. Lost Item Reporting: Standardized self-service submission form for misplaced belongings. 
      3. Found Item Posting with Photos: Easy posting interface allowing finders to upload pictures, descriptions, and discovery locations. 
      4. Automated Cross-Referencing: Algorithmic matching between active lost reports and newly logged found items based on category, date, and campus location. 
      5. Search & Multi-Attribute Filtering: Comprehensive search by category, color, item size, campus sector, and date. 
      6. Proof of Ownership Claim Submission: Structured form capturing claimant identification, specific item marks, and proof attachments. 
      7. Direct In-App Contact & Coordination: Private messaging channel between finder and approved claimant to schedule a campus meetup. 
      8. Digital Turnover Confirmation: One-click mutual handoff confirmation button updating item status to REUNITED . 
      9. Transparent User Transaction History: Dedicated profile ledger recording all surrendered and claimed belongings with counterparty identities. 
      10. Falsely Claimed Item Dispute Reporting: Dispute filing mechanism allowing the true owner to contest false claims and trace the claimant's identity. 
      11. Post-Return Feedback & Ratings: Mutual rating system (1 to 5 stars) building community trust. 


Delimitations (System Boundaries)
      1. No Admin / Staff Intermediaries: The system is completely decentralized; all claim approvals, messaging, handoffs, and dispute communications are handled directly between users.
      2. Mandatory In-Person Handover: Items cannot be shipped or delivered digitally; physical inspection and handover on university grounds are mandatory.
      3. Campus Premise Limitation: Strictly restricted to items lost or found within PUP Biñan Campus boundaries.
      4. Internet Dependency: Requires active internet access; offline caching and standalone SMS fallback are out of scope.
      5. Civil & Disciplinary Recourse: The system provides transparent identity logs and dispute reports;actual disciplinary actions or physical item recovery involving uncooperative false claimants rely on university student affairs or campus security using the platform's audit trail.

Features and Functionalities Beyond the Scope
Mandatory In-Person Handover
While the system handles posting, searching, and claim requests, the final return of the item cannot be completed online. The finder and the claimant must meet in person on campus to hand over the item.
Campus-Only Coverage
The system is restricted exclusively to items that are lost or found within PUP Biñan Campus grounds. Reporting items misplaced outside the campus is not supported.
Internet Dependency
The web application requires active internet access (Wi-Fi or mobile data). It does not support offline use or SMS-only access for users without internet connectivity.
Honesty-Based Verification
The system relies on user-submitted information and the finder's judgment. It cannot automatically confirm that a claimant is the true owner. The Proof of Ownership form and the Transaction History only help reduce false claims.
No Admin Intervention
The system has no admin or staff to settle disputes or remove false posts. A reported false claim only reveals the claimant's identity to the true owner. Returning the item depends on the cooperation of the people involved.
Email-Only Notifications
Alerts are sent by email and in-app notifications only. SMS notifications are not included because of cost and feasibility.
Limited Resources
The project is built by a small student team using free hosting and database plans. Storage, bandwidth, and email limits may restrict the number of photos and users the system can handle.

PURPOSE AND SCOPE
The purpose of this document is to identify, analyze, and document the business and user requirements of Project SEENta, a centralized, peer-to-peer (P2P) web-based lost and found management system developed specifically for the Polytechnic University of the Philippines: Biñan Campus. This document provides a high-level overview of the system’s business needs, objectives, operational scope, and major functionalities to establish a unified understanding among project stakeholders and serve as the baseline for system architecture, design, and implementation.
Unlike traditional lost-and-found processes that depend on a central security office or disorganized social media group chats, SEENta operates on a community-driven model where campus members interact directly. The system covers three primary situational user roles: Finder, Claimant, and Original Owner, all operating under verified institutional identities (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph for students and @pup.edu.ph for faculty/staff).
________________


HIGH-LEVEL FEATURES
Platform Core & Discovery
      1. Institutional User Authentication
 Restricts account registration and access strictly to verified PUP Biñan institutional webmail domains (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph and @pup.edu.ph), requiring legal full name, student/faculty ID number, and active mobile contact number to eliminate anonymous fraudulent accounts.
      2. Lost Item Reporting
 Provides a self-service submission form allowing users who misplaced personal belongings to file an active lost report containing detailed physical descriptions, item category, date/time lost, and last known campus location.
      3. Found Item Posting with Photo Upload
 Allows campus finders to publish discovered items with high-resolution photographic evidence, category tagging, physical condition notes, date/time discovered, and specific campus discovery spot.
      4. Automated Cross-Referencing Engine
 Asynchronously cross-references newly posted found items against active lost reports based on matching categories, dates, and campus location keywords, dispatching automated match alerts to potential owners.
      5. Multi-Attribute Search and Filtering
 Enables users to quickly query campus listings using keywords and granular filters such as color, item size, category, discovery date, and specific campus building/zone.
Claim & Handover Management
         6. Proof of Ownership Claim Workflow
 Replaces administrative gatekeepers with a structured, challenge-response form where claimants submit identifying marks, serial numbers, concealed contents, and proof attachments (receipts or past photos) along with a mandatory honesty declaration.
         7. Claim Review and Verification
 Provides the finder with a private review dashboard to evaluate claimant submissions, allowing them to approve or reject claims based on the accuracy of provided evidence.
         8. Direct In-App Coordination Channel
 Opens a private, secure communication channel between the finder and approved claimant once a claim is accepted, enabling them to safely schedule an in-person campus handover.
         9. Mutual Digital Turnover Confirmation
 Requires both the finder and claimant to click a mutual turnover acknowledgment button during the physical exchange, updating the item state to REUNITED / RETURNED and preventing unilateral status manipulation.
Accountability & Trust
            10. Immutable User Transaction History
 Automatically records every surrendered, claimed, and returned item into a permanent ledger displayed on each user's profile, showing item details, date, location, and the counterparty's verified institutional identity.
            11. Falsely Claimed Item Dispute Reporting
 Empowers an original owner to contest a completed handover by submitting proof of true ownership. Upon validation, the item is flagged as DISPUTED and reveals the verified institutional identity and contact info of the false claimant for recovery purposes.
            12. Post-Return Feedback and Rating System
 Collects optional post-handover star ratings (1 to 5 stars) and brief feedback from both participants to foster peer-to-peer accountability and community trust across the campus.
            13. Real-Time Notification Engine
 Dispatches automated in-app alerts and institutional SMTP email notifications for claim submissions, approvals, rejections, coordination updates, potential matches, and dispute filings.
            14. Campus Accessibility and High-Contrast Mode
 Features a mobile-first, responsive interface equipped with high-contrast display profiles and scalable typography to ensure legibility under harsh, direct outdoor sunlight on campus.

Technical Approach
1. Architecture & Methodology
•      Decoupled client-server: the React app and Node.js API are separate codebases that communicate only over HTTPS using versioned JSON endpoints.
•      Modular backend: routes and controllers are grouped by entity (auth, items, claims, transactions, disputes); repositories handle all queries.
•      Agile delivery: seven two-week sprints in three phases: posting and identity, claiming and handover, then transparency and notifications.
2. Configuration & Security
•      Secrets: kept in git-ignored .env files, loaded through one central config, with separate keys per environment.
•      Registration: only @iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph and @pup.edu.ph emails, validated by regex on both client and server, then confirmed with a one-time password (OTP) sent by email.
•      Authentication: hashed passwords and expiring session tokens.
•      Ownership-based access: only the finder can approve a claim, only the approved claimant can chat, only the original owner can file a dispute. Supabase Row-Level Security enforces this in the database.
•      Input protection: parameterized queries against SQL injection, sanitization against XSS, upload type and size limits.
•      Transport: HTTPS only, CORS limited to the SEENta origin, rate limiting on login, posting, and claim routes.
•      Integrity and privacy: transaction and dispute records are insert-only. Consent to the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) is captured at sign-in, and contact details appear only after claim approval.
3. Performance
•      Paginated item feed and database indexes on category, date, location, and status.
•      Browser-side caching of static lists such as categories and campus locations.
•      Photos compressed before upload to fit free storage and bandwidth limits.
•      Matching runs once per new post, only against active lost reports.

Code lives on GitHub in two branches: dev for active work and main for the protected, production-ready version. A Pull Request from dev to main needs three approvals:

Conceptual Architecture Diagram 
  











































The Conceptual Architecture Diagram illustrates the high-level architecture of SEENta, a web-based Lost and Found Management System for PUP Biñan Campus. The system follows a client-server architecture composed of a Presentation Layer, Business/API Layer, Data Layer, and External Services. These layers work together to allow students and faculty to report lost or found items, search listings, submit ownership claims, communicate with other users, and confirm the return of belongings.
External Users include students and faculty who access SEENta through the web application. They can create lost-item reports, publish found-item posts, search for items, submit proof of ownership, exchange messages, and confirm completed handovers.
Presentation Layer — Frontend provides the user interface and displays the system's features. The proposed frontend uses React, Vite, and Tailwind CSS. It allows users to navigate listings, manage claims, communicate with other users, receive notifications, and view transaction history.
Business/API Layer — Backend handles the application's main processing through a Node.js REST API. It manages authentication, input validation, access control, lost-and-found records, ownership claims, messaging, and handover processing. The frontend communicates with the backend through HTTPS requests and responses.
Data Layer stores the application's persistent information using PostgreSQL through Supabase. It includes user records, lost-item reports, found-item posts, ownership claims, messages, and transaction records. Database operations are handled by the backend to support consistent processing and controlled access.
External Services support additional system functions. Supabase Storage stores item photos and attachments, Supabase Realtime supports live updates, and an SMTP email service delivers verification messages and system notifications.
Together, these components provide the main structure of SEENta and show how users, application processing, data storage, and supporting services interact within the system.


Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD)
The Entity-Relationship Diagram details how data flows and interconnects across Project SEENta to support student-driven item recovery at PUP Biñan. At the core of the system, the users entity authenticates campus members via verified student credentials, linking each account to their submitted inquiries in lost_item_reports and timely operational updates in notifications. The discovery pipeline begins when a finder logs an unattended item into found_item_posts, attaching visual proof through child records in post_photos. Interested owners initiate the retrieval process by submitting proof_of_ownership_claims, which bundles proprietary descriptions and uploaded documentation from claim_attachments while provisioning an isolated communication channel in messages for direct claimant-finder coordination. Once ownership is validated and the physical item is handed over at an authorized campus safe zone, the resolution is finalized as a permanent turnover logged in transaction_records. Should a handover be contested or wrongly disbursed, the case escalates into dispute_reports alongside supplementary evidentiary files in dispute_attachments. By combining relational foreign keys with strict unique constraints, this architecture preserves end-to-end auditability, prevents duplicated claim resolutions, and maintains reliable accountability across the entire campus recovery workflow.  


SOLUTION ASSUMPTIONS, CONSTRAINTS, AND DEPENDENCIES 


Solution Assumptions
               * Verified University Membership: It is assumed that users have access to valid PUP institutional email accounts under the approved domains, such as iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph for students and pup.edu.ph for faculty. These accounts are used to limit system access to legitimate members of the PUP Biñan Campus community.
               * Internet and Device Availability: Students and faculty are assumed to have access to an internet-connected device such as a smartphone, laptop, tablet, or desktop computer. Since SEENta is a web-based application, an active internet connection is required to report items, submit claims, receive updates, and access transaction records.
               * Accurate and Honest User Information: The system assumes that users provide truthful and accurate information when creating accounts, posting lost or found items, and completing Proof of Ownership Claim Forms. The system assists in verifying ownership, but the final decision to approve a claim remains with the finder. 
               * User Cooperation During Handover: It is assumed that the finder and approved claimant are willing to communicate and meet physically within the PUP Biñan Campus to complete the return of an item. Both parties are expected to confirm the completed turnover through the system.
               * Availability of Supporting Services: It is assumed that the selected database, hosting platform, email service, and other supporting online services remain available during normal system operation. The project is expected to use free or student-accessible service plans where possible.


System Constraints
               * Campus-Only Operation: Project SEENta is limited to lost and found items within the PUP Biñan Campus. Items lost or found outside the campus are not covered by the system. 
               * Mandatory Physical Handover: The system cannot physically return or deliver an item. After a claim is approved, the finder and claimant must meet in person on campus to inspect and hand over the item.
               * No Administrative Intermediary: The system does not include an administrator or staff member who approves claims, handles items, or resolves disputes. Claim approval, communication, handover, and dispute coordination are performed directly between users.
               * Limited Ownership Verification: SEENta cannot automatically determine whether a claimant is the real owner of an item. The Proof of Ownership Claim Form provides supporting evidence, but the finder is still responsible for reviewing the submitted information and deciding whether to approve or reject the claim.
               * Internet Dependency: The application requires Wi-Fi or mobile data to function. Offline operation and SMS-only access are outside the current scope of the system.
               * Email and In-App Notifications Only: Notifications are limited to email and in-app alerts. SMS notifications are excluded from the current implementation because of cost and feasibility limitations.
               * Limited Hosting and Storage Resources: Because the project is being developed by a student team using free or limited service plans, storage capacity, bandwidth, uploaded image sizes, email limits, and the total number of supported users may be restricted.
               * No Guaranteed Recovery After a False Claim: SEENta can identify the user who received a disputed item and preserve the transaction record, but it cannot physically force that person to return the item. Actual recovery depends on user cooperation or assistance from appropriate university authorities outside the system.
System Dependencies
               * Internal System Dependencies:
               * The Web Application / Frontend depends on the backend to process registration, item reports, searches, claims, handover confirmations, transaction history, and dispute requests.
               * The Claim Processing feature depends on existing found item posts and authenticated user accounts.
               * The Handover and Transaction History features depend on an approved Proof of Ownership Claim before a turnover can be confirmed.
               * The False Claim Dispute feature depends on an existing completed transaction record so the system can identify the user who previously received the item. 


               * Database Dependency: The system requires a persistent database to store user accounts, lost and found item records, claims, transaction histories, dispute reports, and related system information. The current technical strategy identifies Supabase as a possible database solution, although the final database platform is still subject to confirmation. 
               * Email / Notification Service: SEENta depends on a notification service to deliver important updates such as possible item matches, claim submissions, approvals, rejections, and dispute notifications. If the email service becomes unavailable, email notifications may be delayed even though the rest of the application remains operational. 
               * Hosting Service: The web application depends on a hosting or deployment platform to make SEENta accessible online. The current project strategy identifies services such as Vercel, Netlify, or GitHub as possible hosting options.
               * Institutional Email Availability: User registration depends on the availability of valid PUP institutional email accounts. Users without an approved university email domain cannot create an authorized SEENta account.
               * Network Connectivity: Communication between users, the web application, database, and notification services depends on reliable internet connectivity. A network outage may temporarily prevent users from submitting reports, viewing listings, receiving notifications, or confirming transactions.

Overview


Project SEENta is a centralized, peer-to-peer (P2P) web application designed for the Polytechnic University of the Philippines: Biñan Campus to replace informal verbal inquiries and social media posts. The platform enables verified students and faculty to post found items, file lost reports, verify ownership through structured claim forms, and coordinate direct on-campus handovers. Every exchange is recorded in a tamper-proof Transaction History, establishing community accountability and enabling original owners to dispute false claims without administrative bottlenecks.


Scope:


               * Official PUP email registration and sign-in (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph) (@pup.edu.ph)
               * Lost item reporting
               * Found item posting with photo upload
               * Item search and filtering (category, color, size, location)
               * Automated matching between lost and found items
               * Proof of ownership claim form submission
               * Claim approval and rejection by the finder
               * In-app chat between finder and claimant
               * Mutual turnover confirmation
               * User transaction history recording
               * Report falsely claimed item
               * Feedback and rating after item return
               * Email and in-app notifications


Out of scope:
               * Central office or staff physical item storage
               * Reporting items lost or found outside PUP Biñan Campus
               * Item delivery or courier shipping services
               * Public account registration and personal emails
               * Social media account sign-in (Google, Facebook, Apple)
               * Monetary rewards, tips, or finder fees
               * Automatic physical verification of ownership
               * Disciplinary actions and physical item recovery
               * SMS notifications
               * Offline system usage

Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD)
  



The Entity-Relationship Diagram details how data flows and interconnects across Project SEENta to support student-driven item recovery at PUP Biñan. At the core of the system, the users entity authenticates campus members via verified student credentials, linking each account to their submitted inquiries in lost_item_reports and timely operational updates in notifications. The discovery pipeline begins when a finder logs an unattended item into found_item_posts, attaching visual proof through child records in post_photos. Interested owners initiate the retrieval process by submitting proof_of_ownership_claims, which bundles proprietary descriptions and uploaded documentation from claim_attachments while provisioning an isolated communication channel in messages for direct claimant-finder coordination. Once ownership is validated and the physical item is handed over at an authorized campus safe zone, the resolution is finalized as a permanent turnover logged in transaction_records. Should a handover be contested or wrongly disbursed, the case escalates into dispute_reports alongside supplementary evidentiary files in dispute_attachments. By combining relational foreign keys with strict unique constraints, this architecture preserves end-to-end auditability, prevents duplicated claim resolutions, and maintains reliable accountability across the entire campus recovery workflow.

In this DFD, university stakeholders—specifically verified students and faculty members—are shown connecting to the main registration and authentication interface to submit their institutional credentials. From a technological standpoint, the system processes raw credential data, querying the User accounts data store to validate the @pup.edu.ph domain suffix, enforce single sign-on constraints, and establish an active authenticated session context.
Once authenticated, the system routes user interactions based on intent: users either log an unattended item they discovered via Post found item or report missing personal property via Submit lost item report. In both ingestion streams, raw user inputs are structured into standardized Item details packages and committed directly to the persistent Item database. Technologically, the ingestion of a found item automatically triggers an asynchronous cross-referencing loop against existing lost property reports. If a potential correlation is identified, the system immediately dispatches a Match notification to the reporting party, prompting them to initiate a claim; otherwise, the listing remains active in the searchable campus feed.
When a potential owner identifies a candidate article—either through the automated match notification or through the manual Search & Claim interface—they generate a formal claim package by completing the asynchronous Proof of Ownership form. From a technological standpoint, this form serializes granular, non-public identifying details into structured Claim details, which are persisted in the Claim database and concurrently routed to the private dashboard of the Finder (item poster). The finder evaluates the claim; if rejected, the claim is terminated, leaving the listing active for other claimants. If approved, the process updates the post status, sending real-time notifications to both parties and facilitating a secure campus meeting.
Upon meeting at an official campus safe zone, both users execute mutual physical verification, confirming institutional identity cards and simultaneously triggering turnover acknowledgments in-app. The system captures these synchronized confirmations, immutably committing a finalized record to the Transaction history data store and closing the active custody cycle. In the event of a fraudulent turnover, an Original owner can submit evidence to the Report falsely claimed item process, which queries the Transaction history to retrieve the verified identity and student number of the fraudulent claimant, flags the item as Disputed, and generates an unalterable audit package for formal academic and campus disciplinary recovery.


Key Component Roles  
                     * Claimant (Student/Faculty): Institutional user who submits specific answers (identifying features, serial numbers, wallpapers) through the Proof of Ownership form.
                     * Finder (Student/Faculty): Institutional user retaining custody of the found item, evaluating submitted proofs, and executing handoff.
                     * Client Application (Web / Mobile Frontend): Handles client-side input validation, authentication headers, and reactive UI state updates.
                     * ClaimController: Backend API service handling claim validation, concurrency locking, and status mutations.
                     * TransactionController: Atomic service handling mutual turnover verification, immutable transaction records, and ledger hashing.
                     * Database (Supabase / PostgreSQL): Relational store enforcing Row-Level Security (RLS) and referential integrity.
                     * NotificationService: Publishes real-time push events and alerts to claimant and finder dashboards.


  

1. Domain Entities 
                     * User: Encapsulates verified PUP community identities (@iskolarngbayan.pup.edu.ph / @pup.edu.ph webmail, student/employee numbers, and roles) to restrict system access to institutional members.
                     * FoundItemPost: Tracks found property metadata (category, photo, campus location) and manages lifecycle states (AvailableForClaim to Reunited).
                     * ProofOfOwnershipClaim: Replaces administrative gatekeepers with an asynchronous challenge-response form, storing claimants' identifying answers and evidence.
                     * TransactionRecord: An immutable, dual-sided ledger storing mutual completion signatures, physical location, and a cryptographic hash of both parties' institutional IDs.
                     * DisputeReport: Handles false-claim escalation by linking the immutable transaction log to campus authorities (Office of Student Affairs) for real-world resolution.
2. Controllers & Services
                     * AuthController: Enforces Google SSO domain validation and issues role-based session tokens.
                     * ItemController: Manages lost/found post creation, category filtering, and campus feed discovery.
                     * ClaimController: Coordinates proof submissions, prevents concurrent claims, and handles finder approvals/rejections.
                     * TransactionController: Requires mutual, dual-sided confirmation from both parties before atomically committing the transaction to the ledger.
                     * DisputeController: Aggregates verified claimant data from the ledger into an unalterable audit trail for campus arbitration.
                     * NotificationService: Broadcasts real-time push and WebSocket updates for claims, chat scheduling, and turnover receipts.
